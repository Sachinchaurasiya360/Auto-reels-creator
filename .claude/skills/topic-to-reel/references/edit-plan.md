# The edit plan: think like the editor

An editor never starts with "what graphic goes here". They start with "what does the viewer feel at this second, and what makes them stay for the next one". Write `EDIT_PLAN.md` before any asset or scene exists. It is the cheapest place to fix a boring reel.

## 1. Find the three spikes first

Every retention graph of a good reel has three spikes. Design them before anything else, then fill the space between.

| Spike | When | What it must do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | 0-1.5s | The subject is on screen and moving at frame 0. The image alone asks a question. Muted viewers get it from the picture. |
| Midpoint interrupt | 40-55% of the runtime | The biggest visual change in the reel: the colour world flips, the camera crashes in, the scale changes 3x, a real receipt slams in. It lands on the spoken re-hook. |
| Payoff | last 15-25% | The answer the hook promised, shown as one unforgettable image. Then the loop line. |

If you can't describe each spike in one sentence that a stranger would find interesting, the reel isn't ready to build.

## 2. Beat sheet format

One row per beat (a beat is 1.5-3s, usually one spoken phrase). Use the Scribe word timings for the times.

```
## B07  32.10-35.60  "Binance ke hi system se 50 transaction pehle se sign"
idea:      50 blank cheques fanned out, each getting the same signature
receipt?:  no (metaphor)
focal:     the signature landing on the top cheque
hierarchy: "50" huge (hero), "transactions" medium, "pehle se sign" small italic
change:    fan spreads on "50" (33.08), signature writes on "sign" (34.74)
camera:    slow push 100 -> 106% across the beat
sound:     paper riffle on the fan, pen scratch on the signature
handoff:   the top cheque flies up and becomes the "key" in B08
```

Every row fills every field. A field you can't fill is a beat you haven't thought through.

## 3. Choosing the idea for a beat

Ask in this order and take the first that works:

1. **Is there a receipt?** A real headline, tweet, on-chain transaction, chart, filing, product screenshot. Real proof beats any illustration and is what makes a news reel feel trustworthy. Show it, then direct the eye: zoom into the line that matters, highlight it, blur the rest.
2. **Is there a physical metaphor?** Turn the abstract into an object the viewer has held: signed blank cheques for pre-signed transactions, a master key for admin access, a vault for a hot wallet, a fishing hook for phishing, sandbags for effort. Surreal combinations are good (a laptop hanging from a crane hook, a vault door made of a keyboard): the slight wrongness makes people look twice.
3. **Is there a person?** People watch people. A cut-out photo of a person doing the action (an engineer at a desk, a hand clicking a link) holds attention better than any object. Never generate a real, named person; use a press photo with credit or a name card instead (`assets.md`).4. **Is it a number?** Then the number is the hero, at hero size, with one object that gives it scale (6.3 billion dollars as a stack of cash taller than a building, not as a bar chart).
5. **Only then, type alone.** A single word on screen (`WHY?`) is a legitimate punch beat, at most twice per reel.

Never: a labelled icon that repeats what the voice just said. "Laptop" spoken + laptop icon + the word LAPTOP is three copies of one idea.

## 4. The change ladder

Something on screen changes every 1.5-3 seconds. Use a different rung each time, and save the top rungs for the spikes:

| Rung | Change | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | A word builds in (kinetic type) | constantly; it's the base rhythm |
| 2 | A new element enters the existing frame | most beats |
| 3 | Camera move: push-in 100-108%, or a drift across the stage | sustain a long beat |
| 4 | Punch-in: hard cut to 115-130% on a detail | emphasis on a key word |
| 5 | New frame: hard cut or match cut to a new composition | new idea |
| 6 | Object transition: an element grows into the next scene, or carries across | linking cause and effect |
| 7 | World change: background colour flips, scale jumps 3x, black out | midpoint interrupt, once or twice |

A reel that only uses rungs 1, 2 and 5 feels like slides. Two cuts in a row on the same rung feel like a template.

## 5. Rhythm and pacing

- Cut on the word, not between words. A new image lands on the stressed syllable of its keyword (take it from the word timing, minus 1-2 frames).
- Vary beat length: 1.2s, 2.6s, 1.8s, 3.0s. Equal beats read as a machine.
- Hold the payoff. After the fastest stretch, give the key image 0.4-0.8s of stillness before the reveal; contrast is what makes it hit.
- A pause in the voice is a gift: fill it with a visual action (a door swings, a number lands) instead of dead air.
- J-cut the sound: the next scene's sound starts 2-4 frames before its picture. L-cut on the loop: the last image holds under the first word.
- The last frame should match the first so the loop is invisible.

## 6. Hierarchy per frame

In every frame, decide the reading order before laying anything out: **1 focal subject** (image or hero word), **1 supporting line**, at most **1 small connector**. If there are four things competing, cut one. The viewer's eye should land in under 300ms and know where to go next.

## 7. Retention devices worth planning

- **Open loop on screen**: tease the payoff visually in the first 5s (a blurred, cropped corner of the payoff image) and pay it off later.
- **Progress cue** for process stories (how it happened, steps): a compact, designed indicator of where we are, never a slide counter.
- **Pattern interrupt** at the midpoint (§1).
- **Re-hook text**: the midpoint line ("but here's the scary part") gets the reel's second-biggest type moment.
- **Receipts** at the moment of the boldest claim: proof right when the viewer starts to doubt.
- **Payoff in one image** that's worth a screenshot, which drives saves.
- **Loop seam**: the last line runs into the first.
